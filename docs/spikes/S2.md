# Spike S2: the test pattern filmed by phones (INTERIM)

Status: **INTERIM** (2026-11-17). The six recordings were analysed with the M2 vision code on one
machine. The M2 geometry chain found the code in too few captures for the S2 bars to be judged. This
report gives what was measured, as lower bounds rather than verdicts. S2 is run again once the
geometry stream has fixed location on real optics. **The default mode is unchanged.**

The recordings stay on the owner's machine. Nothing from them (frames, crops or derived media) is in
the repository, and they are named here only generically, as in S1.md section 12.

## 1. Summary

**Main finding:** the M2 chain located the code in **4,191 of 20,848 captures** across the six
recordings. Every located capture also had its header read, was placed in its pattern segment and had
a mesh fitted. All the losses are in the detector (`FinderDetector.Detect`), at the confirmation step:
a quad is reported only when a header decodes and each finder lies where the anchors and the other
three finders put it. There are two classes of failure.

- **Rescaled playback** (Phone A, recording 1, where the pattern played in a player window rather
  than full screen).
  - The finder scan finds all four finders.
  - The header decodes, and most anchors are found.
  - The leave-one-out check then rejects the right-hand finders. They lie 0.55-0.78 cells from where
    the anchors and the other finders put them, against a tolerance of 0.5 cell (one module on a
    135-row grid).
  - The located anchors reach only x = 206 of 240 cells, so those finders are extrapolated.
- **The laptop recordings** (Phone A, recording 3, and Phone B, recordings 4-6).
  - The finder scan finds all four finders, but the header copies are mostly sampled in the wrong
    place: their soft bits are strong but do not decode, and some copies read nothing.
  - Where a header does decode and the anchors span the code, finder centres sit 1-2.5 cells from
    where the anchors put them.
  - The 8-bit recording 6 fails in the same way as the HLG ones, so HLG is not the only cause.
  - **The cause is not yet established.** A bias of the finder centres against the screen's dark
    bezel is one candidate; the header model's lens fit is another.

Only Phone A, recording 2 (a 4K camera filming the 1920x1080 external monitor, 7-21 camera pixels per
cell) is read well: 83% of its captures were located.

**Residuals** (DESIGN.md section 10, step 7 and section 15), on the captures that were located:

1. **Overconfidence, partly mixed and clean.** Real captures are far more overconfident than the
   simulator's. Gray4, pooled over the recordings:
   - partly mixed cells: up to **1,190 times** the stated error rate (bucket 3);
   - cells wholly one frame: up to **1,604 times**.

   About 0.2-0.6% of the most confident bits (|s| of 102 and more, stated below 1e-11) are wrong. On
   recording 2, the best-read one, Gray4's worst bucket is 41 times (clean) and 65 times (partly
   mixed). The simulator's tripod residual (11.08 times at its worst) is therefore not the binding
   one on real data.
2. **Clean-cell underconfidence.** None was seen. The least confident bucket is at or above the stated
   rate (1.0-2.3 times) everywhere.
3. **Wrong credit and yield.** The true weight is unknown on real captures. As a stand-in, the share of
   wrong credited symbols that equal a neighbouring frame's symbol is at the level chance alone gives,
   for every recording and modulation (for example 45.5% against 43.8% for Gray4 on recording 2).
   There is therefore no sign of cells credited to the wrong frame. Yield (the mean clean share per
   capture) is 0.29-0.37 on recording 2, whose camera runs at or below the display rate in most
   segments, and 0.81 on recording 3.
4. **Glare.** No recording shows glare. The most the equaliser flagged in any segment is 7.8% of cells
   (recording 2, 60 fps display, 4-pixel cells, RGB8, with a bit error rate of 0.33, so mostly a
   misread code rather than glare). Clipping by the sampler stays under 0.3% everywhere.

## 3. Method

`go run ./spikes/pattern -analyse` (spikes/pattern/README.md) decodes a recording with the system
ffmpeg, at the source's own bit depth, with colour. It runs every capture through the M2 chain:
- the finder detector, with no previous quad, so that captures can be analysed in any order;
- the header reader, the mesh fitter and the footprint sampler;
- `vision.SamplePalette`;
- `AnchorPhase` with the palette's gamma;
- `TileEqualiser` with stripe cancellation;
- `SoftBits` for each phase.

The pattern's data cells carry only their scramble bits, so each capture's header gives the code
frame's index, and every expected bit is recomputed from it (FORMAT.md section 9). A phase is matched
to a code frame as follows:
- the frame whose header shows that phase;
- otherwise the frame one step before or after the first header's frame;
- otherwise (two steps away, which is ambiguous) the phase is skipped.

For each segment of the pattern (display rate, cell size, modulation):

- **Located:** captures located, placed in the segment by their header and fitted, against the captures
  taken while the segment showed. A capture is placed by its time plus the recording's offset into the
  pattern, the median over the located captures of pattern time less capture time.
- **Camera/display:** the camera's frame rate over the display rate.
- **Px/cell:** camera pixels per cell at the code's centre, from the mesh (median over the captures).
- **Clean per capture:** the share of data cells the phase field credits (weight at least 0.92).
- **Clean per code frame:** the share of a frame's cells that some capture credited, averaged over the
  code frames shown while a located capture was exposed.
- **Clipped:** cells the sampler marked saturated. **Glare:** cells the equaliser then flagged.
- **Delivered bits per cell:** per code frame, the soft bits of every capture crediting a cell are
  summed per bit, as a decoder combining captures would. The figure is b x the share of bits stated x
  (1 - H2(error rate among the stated bits)), the capacity of a binary channel with erasures and
  errors. A cell no capture credited delivers nothing.
  - **Located:** averaged over the code frames shown while a located capture was exposed. This is
    the channel's figure given that the code was found.
  - **All shown:** averaged over every frame shown while the segment was filmed.
- **Net rate:** delivered bits per cell (located) x data cells per frame x display rate x 0.75, against
  the recording's bitrate (file size over duration).

The confidence tables are those of the honesty tests (ten buckets of |s|). They are split by the phase
field's own weight: "partly mixed" means a credited cell with an estimated weight below 0.999, since the
true weight is unknown on real captures. A ratio of observed to stated error rate is given only for
buckets of at least 10,000 bits stating at least 10 errors.

The analysis of all six recordings takes **about 16 minutes** on this machine with 14 workers (the
tool's `-workers`):
- 1080p recordings: about 90 s each;
- 4K recordings: 3-4.5 minutes each;
- peak memory: about 3.5 GB.

To reproduce, from the repository root with the recordings in `recordings/s2/`:

```sh
go run ./spikes/pattern -analyse recordings/s2/<file> -patterns recordings/patterns \
    -label "Phone A, recording 1" -json rec1.json -workers 14
go run ./spikes/pattern -summarise rec1.json rec2.json rec3.json rec4.json rec5.json rec6.json
```
