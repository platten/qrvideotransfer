# Spike S2: the test pattern filmed by phones (INTERIM)

Status: **INTERIM** (2026-11-17). The six recordings were analysed with the M2 vision code on one
machine. The M2 geometry chain located the code in too few captures for the S2 bars to be judged. This
report gives what was measured; section 1 says which figures are lower bounds and which are
conditional on location. S2 is run again once the geometry stream has fixed location on real optics.
**The default mode is unchanged.**

The recordings stay on the owner's machine. Nothing from them (frames, crops or derived media) is in
the repository, and they are named here only generically, as in S1.md section 12.

## 1. Summary

### Main finding 1: location

The M2 chain located the code in **4,191 of 20,848 captures** across the six recordings. Every
located capture also had its header read, was placed in its pattern segment and had a mesh fitted. All
the losses are in the detector (`FinderDetector.Detect`), at its confirmation step: a quad is reported
only when a header decodes and each finder lies where the anchors and the other three finders put it.
There are two classes of failure.

- **Rescaled playback** (Phone A, recording 1, where the pattern played in a player window rather
  than full screen).
  - The finder scan finds all four finders, the header decodes, and most anchors are found.
  - The leave-one-out check then rejects the right-hand finders. They lie 0.55-0.78 cells from where
    the anchors and the other finders put them, against a tolerance of 0.5 cell (one module on a
    135-row grid).
  - The located anchors reach only x = 206 of 240 cells, so those finders are extrapolated.
- **The laptop recordings** (Phone A, recording 3, and Phone B, recordings 4-6).
  - The finder scan finds all four finders, but the header copies are mostly sampled in the wrong
    place: their soft bits are strong but do not decode, and some copies read nothing.
  - Where a header does decode and the anchors span the code, finder centres sit 1-2.5 cells from
    where the anchors put them.
  - The 8-bit recording 6 fails in the same way as the HLG ones, so HLG is not the only cause.
  - **The cause is not yet established.** A bias of the finder centres against the screen's dark
    bezel is one candidate; the header model's lens fit is another.

Only Phone A, recording 2 (a 4K camera filming the 1920x1080 external monitor, 7-21 camera pixels per
cell) is read well: 83% of its captures were located.

### Main finding 2: overconfidence

On real captures the soft bits are overconfident. **The honest-confidence property M2 asserts on the
simulator (DESIGN.md section 15) does not hold on real data.** The extent depends on where in the
code the cells lie (sections 5.1 and 5.2):

- **Whole code.** Gray4, pooled over the recordings, reaches about 16,000-17,000 times the stated
  rate in bucket 4, for cells wholly one frame and for partly mixed cells alike. Per recording, Gray4
  ranges from 475-703 times (recording 2, bucket 4) through 2,353-3,788 times (recording 1, bucket 3) to
  30,673 times (recording 3, bucket 4). 0.2-0.6% of the most confident bits (|s| of 103 and more,
  stated below 1e-11) are wrong.
- **Most of that comes from edge cells of located captures whose mesh is wrong.**
  - On Phone A, recording 3, the left 10% of columns is 38-49% wrong among confident bits in every
    segment, against 0-9e-4 in the inner 60%. That is every capture's left edge.
  - On Phone A, recording 1, the outer 10% of columns on either side is 0.5-22% wrong, against at most
    1.8e-3 in the inner 60%.
- **Interior** (inner 80% of the grid both ways). For Gray4 on recording 1, confident errors fall from
  3.06e-3 to 2.0e-5. On recording 3, the central columns hold 0 Gray2 errors among 3.0 million bits,
  according to the reviewer's probe.
- **Interior overconfidence that cannot yet be attributed.** Over the inner 80% of the grid, the
  largest observed-over-stated ratios (section 5.2) run from about 2 to 38,600 times the stated rate.
  The top of that range is pooled RGB8, partly mixed, bucket 4.
  - **Low end:** recording 2's Gray2 is 1.9-4.6 times and its Gray4 21-48 times.
  - **Everything else:** about 80 to 38,600 times. That is about 7 to 3,500 times the simulator's
    worst accepted residual (11 times, `tripod`'s partly mixed cells), from nearly one to three and a
    half orders of magnitude above it.

  Three examples, for cells wholly one frame:

  | Recording | Modulation | Inner 80% | Central half (reviewer's probe) |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Phone A, recording 1 | Gray4 | 918 times (bucket 3) | bucket 3: 16 errors where 0.2 stated; bucket 2: 21 errors where 1.9 stated, in 5,242 bits |
  | Phone A, recording 3 | Gray4 | 804 times (bucket 3) | not probed |
  | Phone A, recording 2 | RGB8 | about 2,650 times (bucket 3) | not probed |

  - The recording 2 RGB8 figure is as bad over the inner 80% as over the whole code, with a bit error
    rate of 0.26 at 4-pixel cells.
  - RGB8 on recording 3 is 114-133 times over the inner 80%, and 16-42 times at its centre (the
    reviewer's probe).

  **These interior figures are not settled.**

There are three candidate causes. This report does not pick one.

1. **(i) Cells sampled off position** because of the geometry problems of finding 1.
2. **(ii) The equaliser's level model not matching the real tone response** of these phones (HLG,
   tone mapping, colour processing).
3. **(iii) The analysis tool's own expected bits or frame assignment.**

What the measurements say about each:
- **(iii) is ruled out for cells wholly one frame** (section 3.3). Three checks show it:
  - `TestAnalyseSimulated` films the pattern with the simulator and runs the captures through this
    same analysis. Every credited cell is assigned to the frame the simulator showed, and 5.1 million
    bits of cells wholly one frame give no error.
  - The pattern file itself, analysed as a perfect capture, is located in all 6,480 captures, with no
    error in any of its 27 segments. That was run by the reviewer; `TestAnalysePerfectPattern` keeps it
    as a test.
  - So recording 2's RGB8 errors arise between the display and the camera.
- **(iii) is not ruled out for partly mixed cells.** In the same simulation they show an excess that
  is unexplained: 648 errors where 25.1 are stated (26 times, bucket 1) and 270 where 3.67 are stated
  (74 times, bucket 2). Running the honesty test in this regime would separate the causes (section 6).
- **The interior evidence points mainly to (i) for the extremes.**
- **Interior overconfidence remains**, from about 20 to 38,600 times the stated rate, leaving out
  recording 2's Gray2. Recording 2's RGB8, as bad inside as at the edges, points rather at (ii). That
  is not established.

The residual goes to the geometry stream first. Radiometry is judged again after the re-run.
**Section 5.1's per-position table is the acceptance measure for the geometry fix:** after it, the
confident-error rate at the edges should match the interior's on the same recordings. That is a
measured outcome, not a constant to tune.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 The chain

`go run ./spikes/pattern -analyse` (spikes/pattern/README.md) decodes a recording with the system
ffmpeg, at the source's own bit depth, with colour. It runs every capture through the M2 chain:
- the finder detector, with no previous quad, so that captures can be analysed in any order;
- the header reader, the mesh fitter and the footprint sampler;
- `vision.SamplePalette`;
- `AnchorPhase` with the palette's gamma;
- `TileEqualiser` with stripe cancellation;
- `SoftBits` for each phase.

The pattern's data cells carry only their scramble bits, so every expected bit is recomputed from a
code frame's index (FORMAT.md section 9).

### 3.2 Which code frame a capture shows

A capture is assigned to code frames in three steps.

1. **By header:** the header copies give the index of the code frame they show; there may be two when
   two frames are mixed.
2. **By phase:** the phase field credits each cell to a phase (frame index mod 4). A phase is matched
   to:
   - the frame whose header shows that phase;
   - otherwise the frame one step before or after the first header's frame (phase difference 3 or
     1);
   - otherwise (a difference of 2, which is ambiguous) the phase is skipped.

   A frame outside the header's segment is skipped too.
3. **By time:** each capture is also placed in the segment showing at its time plus the recording's
   offset into the pattern. The offset is the median over the located captures of pattern time less
   capture time. Time is used only to count the captures and code frames of a segment, never to
   choose a capture's expected bits.

**How a wrong assignment would show.** If a credited cell were assigned to the wrong code frame, its
expected symbol would be that of an unrelated frame. The two frames' scramble sequences are
independent, so the cell's stated symbol would be wrong about as often as chance gives (3/4 for
Gray4). It would match the true neighbouring frame's symbol. Wrong assignment therefore shows as many
wrong credited symbols that equal a neighbour's, far above the chance level of section 5.4. It does
not show as confident errors spread over every frame. The share measured is at chance level.

One wrong assignment would not show this way: a frame four indices away, at the same phase. Its
symbol is not a neighbour's. That is possible only if a capture spans four or more code frames, which
is unlikely at these rates: at most 60 fps display against a 30 fps camera, so about two frames per
capture.

### 3.3 The analysis on simulated captures (candidate iii)

`TestAnalyseSimulated` (spikes/pattern) renders the pattern's Gray4, 4-pixel, 12 fps code frames. The
simulator films them with its `tripod` preset (1920x1080 camera at 30 fps), and every capture goes
through `-analyse`'s own code. Measured with 96 captures (the long version):
- **Assignment:** every credited cell is assigned to the frame the simulator showed there with that
  phase.
- **Cells wholly one frame:** 5.1 million bits, with no error, whether split by true or by estimated
  weight.
- **Partly mixed cells by true weight:** 648 errors where 25.1 are stated in bucket 1 (26 times,
  2,850 bits), and 270 where 3.67 are stated in bucket 2 (74 times, 8,871 bits). Those buckets are
  below the honesty tests' 10,000 bits, so they are not judged, and CI does not catch them.
  - This excess is **unexplained**. It may be the chain's partly-mixed residual, larger in this regime
    than in the honesty tests' configuration (480x270 grid, 4K cameras), or the analysis.
  - Running the honesty test in this regime would separate the two: Gray4, 4-pixel cells, 12 fps
    display, 30 fps camera. It is a next step (section 6), not run here.
  - The excess is recorded as an open residual in DESIGN.md section 10, step 7.

The test asserts:
- the simulator's bounds (twice the stated rate for cells wholly one frame, 12 times for partly
  mixed ones, with Poisson slack);
- no wrong confident bit.

**The perfect-capture baseline.** `TestAnalysePerfectPattern` takes one code frame of each of the
1920x1080 pattern's 27 segments, rendered and captured head-on at one camera pixel per display pixel,
and runs it through the analysis. Every capture is located, and every bit is stated without error.
The reviewer ran the whole pattern file the same way: all 6,480 captures located, and no error in any
segment, RGB8 at 4-pixel cells included.

**Conclusion.** The analysis manufactures no overconfidence in cells wholly one frame. For partly
mixed cells the question is open.

### 3.4 Figures per segment

For each segment of the pattern (display rate, cell size, modulation):

- **Located:** captures located, placed in the segment by their header and fitted, against the
  captures taken while the segment showed.
- **Camera/display:** the camera's frame rate over the display rate.
- **Px/cell:** camera pixels per cell at the code's centre, from the mesh (median over the captures).
- **Clean per capture:** the share of data cells the phase field credits (weight at least 0.92).
- **Clean per code frame:** the share of a frame's cells that some capture credited, averaged over the
  code frames shown while a located capture was exposed.
- **Clipped:** cells the sampler marked saturated. **Glare:** cells the equaliser then flagged.
- **Delivered bits per cell:** per code frame, the soft bits of every capture crediting a cell are
  summed per bit, as a decoder combining captures would. The figure is b x the share of bits stated x
  (1 - H2(error rate among the stated bits)), the capacity of a binary channel with erasures and
  errors. A cell no capture credited delivers nothing.
  - **Located (given location, selection-biased):** averaged over the code frames shown while a
    located capture was exposed.
  - **All shown (a lower bound):** averaged over every frame shown while the segment was filmed.
- **Net rate:** delivered bits per cell x data cells per frame x display rate x 0.75, against the
  recording's bitrate.

The confidence tables are those of the honesty tests (ten buckets of |s|). They are split by the phase
field's own weight: "partly mixed" means a credited cell with an estimated weight below 0.999, since the
true weight is unknown on real captures. Section 5.2 gives them over the whole code and over the
interior.

### 3.5 Time

The analysis of all six recordings takes **about 16 minutes** on this machine with 14 workers (the
tool's `-workers`):
- 1080p recordings: about 90 s each;
- 4K recordings: 3-4.5 minutes each;
- peak memory: about 3.5 GB.

To reproduce, from the repository root with the recordings in `recordings/s2/`, writing the JSON
beside them:

```sh
go run ./spikes/pattern -analyse recordings/s2/<file> -patterns recordings/patterns \
    -label "Phone A, recording 1" -json recordings/s2/rec1.json -workers 14
go run ./spikes/pattern -summarise recordings/s2/rec1.json recordings/s2/rec2.json \
    recordings/s2/rec3.json recordings/s2/rec4.json recordings/s2/rec5.json recordings/s2/rec6.json
```

- **Located:** captures located against captures taken during the segment.

- **Net:** the estimated net rate, from the given-location figure.
- **Display rates above the camera's.** Where the display runs at or above the camera's rate

- **The 29.98 fps camera** of recording 2 beats slowly against the 30 fps display. So the clean share of

  cells, almost 0 at 8 and 12.
