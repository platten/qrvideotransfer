real-optics geometry fix (#45) on one machine. This replaces the interim report of #44.

**Verdicts:**
- **Bar 1:** met narrowly given location; 1.48 over every frame shown.
- **Bar 2:** met.
- **Bar 3:** met by the formula only; 3.3% at a setting `normal` decodes with margin.

**The default mode and the planning table are unchanged.** Options (a) to (c) below are open questions
that the designer is putting to the owner.

The recordings stay on the owner's machine. Nothing from them (frames, crops or derived media) is in
the repository, and they are named here only generically, as in S1.md section 12.

### 1.4 Are the located real captures decodable?

**Where the camera runs at least twice the display rate, the located captures decode with `normal`.
Where it runs at the display rate, `robust` at best.** This estimate needs no new study:
- The inner code's frame error rate reaches 1e-2 at a channel capacity of about 0.78 bits per sent bit
  for `normal` (code 2, rate 0.685) and 0.48 for `robust` (code 4, rate 0.359). These are the binary-input
  AWGN capacities at the Es/N0 where DESIGN.md section 8 measured FER 1e-2.
- Each segment's delivered bits per cell, given location, divided by the bits per cell, gives its
  capacity per sent bit. Erasures (cells no capture credited) count as zero.
- The threshold assumes errors and erasures are spread across each codeword. Uncredited cells come in
  spatial bands (frame-mixing boundaries), so whether that holds depends on how the inner codewords are
  interleaved across the frame.

Of the 63 segments with at least half their captures located:

- **16 reach 0.78** and would decode with `normal`. All are at 12 fps display (camera/display 2.5),
  except recording 6's Gray2 and Gray4 at 30 fps display (camera/display 2). They span:
  - Gray2 from 3.0 camera pixels per cell (recording 1);
  - Gray4 from 6.4 pixels per cell (also 7.2 on recording 2, at 0.815);
  - RGB8 from 6.4 pixels per cell (recording 1).

  Their bit error rates are 0-3e-2.
- **25 reach 0.48** and would decode with `robust`.
  - This adds recording 1's and recording 2's 30 fps display segments, where the camera runs at the
    display rate. Those reach 0.495-0.755 per sent bit, limited mostly by cells left uncredited. Across
    the 9 segments that `robust` adds, bit error rates are 1.1e-3 to 6.1e-2.
  - It also adds two 12 fps segments: recording 1's Gray4 at 3.2 pixels per cell, and recording 2's
    RGB8.
- **The rest**, mostly where the display runs faster than the camera, would not decode with any
  shipped code.

**The caveat is the overconfidence** (section 1.2). The estimate takes the soft bits at their stated
worth. Real soft bits are overconfident, by 5-60 times in the interior for Gray2 and Gray4 cells wholly
one frame, and confident errors run at 1e-6 to 4e-4. A min-sum LDPC decoder corrects sparse confident
errors, but overconfident wrong bits push it toward a wrong codeword more than honest ones would. So
the 16 segments look decodable where they have margin, which is most of them, at 0.84 to 1.0 per sent
bit. The segments near 0.78 (0.815-0.837) are not safe without calibration.

**Input to the M3 decision:**
- **M3's end-to-end path can be built and run on real recordings now,** at the conservative setting.
  That is `normal`, Gray2 or Gray4 at 6 or more camera pixels per cell, with a 12 fps display or a
  30 fps display filmed by a 60 fps camera. Otherwise it is `robust` with a 30 fps display filmed at
  30 fps. It does not have to wait for radiometry on real data.
- **Radiometry on real data matters for the throughput settings:** calibrating the overconfidence, and
  RGB8. It has to come before those settings can be relied on.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 The chain

`go run ./spikes/pattern -analyse` (spikes/pattern/README.md) decodes a recording with the system
ffmpeg, at the source's own bit depth, with colour. It runs every capture through the vision chain on
main after #45:
- the finder detector, with no previous quad, so that captures can be analysed in any order;
- the header reader, the mesh fitter and the footprint sampler;
- `vision.SamplePalette`;
- `AnchorPhase` with the palette's gamma;
- `TileEqualiser` with stripe cancellation;
- `SoftBits` for each phase.

The pattern's data cells carry only their scramble bits, so every expected bit is recomputed from a
code frame's index (FORMAT.md section 9). The tool is unchanged since #44.

### 3.2 Which code frame a capture shows

A capture is assigned to code frames in three steps.

1. **By header:** the header copies give the index of the code frame they show; there may be two when
   two frames are mixed.
2. **By phase:** the phase field credits each cell to a phase (frame index mod 4). A phase is matched
   to:
   - the frame whose header shows that phase;
   - otherwise the frame one step before or after the first header's frame;
   - otherwise (two steps away, which is ambiguous) the phase is skipped.

   A frame outside the header's segment is skipped too.
3. **By time:** each capture is also placed in the segment showing at its time plus the recording's
   offset into the pattern. The offset is the median over the located captures of pattern time less
   capture time. Time is used only to count a segment's captures and code frames, never to choose a
   capture's expected bits.

**How a wrong assignment would show.** A credited cell assigned to the wrong code frame would be wrong
about as often as chance gives, and its stated symbol would equal the true neighbouring frame's.
Wrong assignment therefore shows as wrong credited symbols matching a neighbour far above the chance
level (section 5.3).

One wrong assignment would not show this way: a frame four indices away, at the same phase. That is
possible only if a capture spans four or more code frames, which is unlikely at these rates.

### 3.3 The analysis checked against known truth (candidate iii)

- **`TestAnalyseSimulated`** films the pattern with the simulator (`tripod`, 1080p30, Gray4, 4-pixel
  cells, 12 fps display) and runs every capture through the analysis.
  - Every credited cell is assigned to the frame the simulator showed.
  - Cells wholly one frame give 5.1 million bits with no error.
  - Partly mixed cells by true weight show an unexplained excess: 648 errors where 25.1 are stated,
    and 270 where 3.67 are stated (26 and 74 times). Those buckets are too small to be judged at
    10,000 bits. This is the open residual of DESIGN.md section 10, step 7.
- **`TestAnalysePerfectPattern`** analyses one rendered code frame of each of the 1920x1080 pattern's
  27 segments as a perfect capture. Every capture is located, and every bit is stated without error.
  The review of #44 ran the whole pattern file the same way: all 6,480 captures located, and no error
  in any segment.

The analysis therefore manufactures no overconfidence in cells wholly one frame. For partly mixed
cells the question stays open.

### 3.4 Figures per segment

For each segment of the pattern (display rate, cell size, modulation):

- **Located:** captures located, placed in the segment by their header and fitted, against the
  captures taken while the segment showed.
- **Camera/display:** the camera's frame rate over the display rate.
- **Px/cell:** camera pixels per cell at the code's centre, from the mesh (median over the captures).
- **Clean per capture:** the share of data cells the phase field credits (weight at least 0.92).
- **Clean per code frame:** the share of a frame's cells that some capture credited, over the code
  frames shown while a located capture was exposed.
- **Clipped:** cells the sampler marked saturated. **Glare:** cells the equaliser then flagged.
- **Delivered bits per cell:** per code frame, the soft bits of every capture crediting a cell are
  summed per bit, as a decoder combining captures would. The figure is b x the share of bits stated x
  (1 - H2(error rate among the stated bits)), the capacity of a binary channel with erasures and
  errors. A cell no capture credited delivers nothing.
  - **Located (given location, selection-biased):** averaged over the code frames shown while a
    located capture was exposed.
  - **All shown (a lower bound):** averaged over every frame shown while the segment was filmed.
- **Per sent bit:** the located figure over b, the capacity per sent bit that section 1.4 sets against
  the codes.
- **Net rate:** delivered bits per cell (located) x data cells per frame x display rate x 0.75,
  against the recording's bitrate.

The confidence tables are those of the honesty tests (ten buckets of |s|). "Partly mixed" means a
credited cell with an estimated weight below 0.999, since the true weight is unknown on real captures.

## 6. What follows

1. **The default mode** (DESIGN.md sections 5 and 13, FORMAT.md section 14) and the planning table
   (DESIGN.md section 11) are unchanged in this PR. The designer takes options (a) to (c) of section 1
   to the owner.
2. **M3's end-to-end path** can be built and run on real recordings at the conservative setting
   (section 1.4): a 12 fps display, or a 30 fps display with a 60 fps camera. It does not have to wait for radiometry on real data.
3. **Radiometry on real data** comes before the throughput settings are relied on:
   - calibrating the interior overconfidence (section 1.2);
   - RGB8;
   - the simulated partly-mixed residual;
   - the honesty test in the regime of section 3.3 (Gray4, 4-pixel cells, 12 fps display, 30 fps
     camera), which separates the analysis from the chain as the source of that residual.
4. **Geometry:** the laptop recordings' corners and recording 1's edges stay as DESIGN.md section 19
   records them.
5. **Re-filming** with a margin around the code is for the designer to raise when the G1 list is drawn
   up.
